## 3. Setting 1 (rank 1): seized exhibits and case property held by police and CNB officers

### Verdict

| Criterion | Score (1 to 5) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Role clarity | 5 | Investigation officer, arresting-team officer, exhibit or property-store keeper, lab (HSA) receiving officer. All appear in the sources above. |
| Process clarity | 4 | Chain of custody is defined by case law and the SPF release describes restitution procedure and acknowledgement receipts. Internal SOPs are not public. |
| Evidence of incidents | 5 | Two officers charged in 2023 (one sentenced 2024), more in 2025, a capital-case acquittal on a handover gap, and a Parliament-reported break in Parti Liyani. |
| Current solution and its gap | 3 | The current Singapore system is not public, so the gap is partly inferred. Vendor tools are fixed-point tools. The documented failure (Affandi) is a handover recorded by only one side. |
| Offline reality | 3 | Singapore has strong coverage. The reason to be offline-first is raids, vehicles, basements, court and lab sites where phones are restricted, and resilience. This is the weakest fit with the challenge wording. [inference] |
| Handovers away from the register | 5 | Affandi is a night-time handover at the scene between named officers, with a pocketbook that was not filled in. |
| Fit with the concept | 5 | Sealed evidence bags with a serial number are discrete, tagged items passed between named people. |
| Defence or DSO link | 3 | Home Team rather than defence. The link is "government-held sensitive items with a legal chain", and the same pattern applies to military police and defence property. [inference] |
| Easy to explain | 5 | "Who held the bag at 8pm?" needs no jargon. |
| **Total** | **38 / 45** | |

### Who the safety personnel are
- Police investigation officers (IOs) and arresting-team officers who seize items, CNB officers, property-store or exhibit officers, forensic and HSA receiving officers. [fact: roles named in the police release and the Court of Appeal judgment]
- Supervisors who audit cases. [fact: "regularly conducts audits of cases" in the 2023 release]

### The security process, with its legal basis
- Evidence law: the Prosecution must prove an unbroken chain of custody beyond reasonable doubt, accounting for each movement from seizure to analysis. [fact: Affandi, https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2018_SGCA_87]
- Criminal law: officers who misappropriate seized money are charged with criminal breach of trust by a public servant. [fact: 2023 release]
- Return of seized property: a Court Order is required in all cases before it is returned. [fact: MHA reply, 3 Feb 2026]
- Statutory seizure and reporting duties (Criminal Procedure Code): not found. I recall the CPC has provisions on seizure and on reporting seized property to a Magistrate, but I did not open the Act and the earlier team notes say Singapore Statutes Online blocked fetching, so treat this as unverified and check before saying a section number on stage.
- Records at the scene: pocketbooks and field diaries. [fact: Affandi]

### The current solution and its gaps
- Paper pocketbooks and field diaries at the scene, plus an internal property register that is not public. [fact for pocketbook and diary; register is an inference]
- Detection is slow and indirect: the two 2023-charged officers were caught through case reviews and financial due diligence, years after the acts (one arrested Jul 2020 for acts to 2017; the other arrested Oct 2020 for acts 2017 to 2020). [fact: 2023 release]
- The gap [inference]: a handover is written down by one side, if at all, and the written account can later conflict with the other side's account (Affandi). In one case the same officer who seized the money controlled the receipts, and the receipts were forged. [fact: forged receipts alleged in the release]

### How our concept applies
- Each evidence bag carries a tamper-evident seal with a serial and QR code. The seizing officer creates the item's token and a second officer co-signs the creation, so the first link does not rest on one person. [inference]
- Every handover (scene to IO, IO to property store, store to HSA or court) is signed by giver and receiver on their phones and swapped by QR, with no network. The receiver scans the seal and confirms it is intact and the contents match (count or value) before signing.
- At sync, the server rebuilds the chain. It flags two handovers from the same giver for the same item (the Affandi pattern: two officers each claiming they handed it over) and flags a bag that appears at the store with no matching link. [inference, following the concept document]
- Cash is handled as a sealed, counted bag, so the token covers the bag, not the money inside. A mismatch shows up as a disagreement at the next signature. [inference]

### One demo moment
Replay the Affandi story in 60 seconds. Two team members each say "I held the bag until 10.47pm" and "I held it until 10.56pm". Show the old way (two stories, a blank pocketbook line). Then show the app: the bag's chain on screen with each signed handover and time, the missing link highlighted. Go online and the server flags "two handovers from the same giver for one item", naming both. Story line: "In 2018 this gap freed two men from a capital charge. Everyone agreed the bundles existed. Nobody could prove who held them."

### Weak spots
- Offline is not the natural story in Singapore; the pitch must lean on "signed at the moment by both sides" rather than "no signal". [inference]
- Collusion: two officers can sign a false handover. The token proves who signed, not that what they signed is true. The first link (seizure) is the weakest, and cash is fungible.
- Several charges are still allegations (the 2025 forgery case, the CNB officer, the 2025 vehicle case). Only the 2024 sentencing is a conviction, and I saw it only in a search summary. Use charged-not-convicted language on stage.
- Selling a police-custody app to a defence lab may feel off-topic; frame it as "any government item with a legal chain of custody" and connect to MINDEF/SAF property. [inference]
- The current SPF system and its gaps are not public, so a judge from government may say "we already have this". Do not claim SPF lacks a system.
- Sensitive: it names police officers in conduct cases. Name roles and cases, not people, on stage.
- Court acceptance of digital signatures for chain of custody: not researched.

## 4. Setting 2 (rank 2): keys and access cards held by security officers

### Verdict

| Criterion | Score (1 to 5) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Role clarity | 4 | Licensed security officers and facility key custodians. Roles exist; the key-custodian duty is company-specific. |
| Process clarity | 2 | Key control is described by vendors and company SOPs; no law or public standard found. |
| Evidence of incidents | 2 | Building staff abusing access (concierge S$2m allegation, condo manager access-card thefts) but no documented case where a key-custody chain itself failed. Dates thin. |
| Current solution and its gap | 4 | Electronic key cabinets are a mature product. Their gap (what happens after the key leaves the cabinet) is clear by design, though no vendor page states it. |
| Offline reality | 3 | Cabinets are built to run standalone. Hand-ons happen in car parks, lifts, rooftops. Singapore coverage is good. |
| Handovers away from the register | 5 | The whole problem is the hand-on away from the cabinet. |
| Fit with the concept | 4 | Keys and cards are discrete items passed between named people. Tamper-evident seals and tags are used for key rings. |
| Defence or DSO link | 3 | Key control is basic to any secure site; Singapore-specific defence cases not found. |
| Easy to explain | 5 | Everyone understands a key handover. |
| **Total** | **32 / 45** | |

### Who the safety personnel are
- PSIA-regulated security officers and agencies. [search summary only: the Act regulates security officers, agencies and service providers; https://sso.agc.gov.sg/act/psia2007]
- Facility or control-room key custodians; shift supervisors who hand over key rings.

### The security process, with its legal basis
- Legal basis for key custody: not found. [inference] it is mostly company SOP and contract. The Penal Code offence of criminal breach of trust applies if a key holder misuses what is entrusted, but I did not open the Act.
- The strongest legal key custody (armoury keys) falls under firearms, which another scout covers.
- Process (typical) [inference]: sign a key out of the cabinet, carry it, return it before shift end; supervisor reviews the log.

### The current solution and its gaps
- Electronic key cabinets (Traka and similar): standalone operation, local audit memory, optional central management. See section 2. [search summary only]
- Gap [inference]: the log ends at the cabinet door. A hand-on to another person, a contractor, or a resident handing a key to staff (the 2024 concierge allegation) is invisible. Store-and-forward behaviour on reconnect: not found.

### How our concept applies
- The cabinet stays the vault. Our token starts when the key leaves: the holder hands it on and both sign; the receiver scans the key tag; a ring that is not returned shows as "out with Officer X for N hours".
- At sync the server merges cabinet logs with handover chains and flags a key that two people claim, or a key in use with no open custody. [inference]
- A resident-key scenario (resident hands a key to the concierge) needs a resident-side signature; the concept expects named, enrolled parties, so this is a stretch. [inference]

### One demo moment
Shift change in a basement car park with no signal. The cabinet log says "Guard A took the master ring at 18:00". The phone chain shows A handed to B at 18:40 and B to a contractor C at 21:00. When the ring goes missing, the chain says who held it last. A cabinet alone cannot show that.

### Weak spots
- Thin law and thin incident evidence; a judge who asks "is this required by law, and has it gone wrong?" gets weak answers.
- Cabinet vendors may already offer hand-on features; I did not verify, and Morse Watchmans was not researched.
- A key is a lower-stakes item than an evidence bag, so it can feel like a toy.
- Overlaps with the armoury-key scenario of the firearms scout; if that is chosen, this setting folds into it.
