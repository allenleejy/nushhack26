## Who the safety personnel are (official titles)

**Auxiliary Police Officer (APO)**
- Employed by an auxiliary police force (for example Certis, AETOS). The police release on the 2024 case names Certis as the employer who alerted police. [fact] https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2024/20240510_man_arrested_for_unlawful_carrying_of_a_firearm_within_two_and_a_half_hours (10 May 2024)
- Legal home: Police Force Act 2004, Part 9 (creation of auxiliary forces s 86; appointment of APOs s 92; Auxiliary Police Forces Regulations under s 104). [search summary only] https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PFA2004 (page returned 403 on fetch; seen via search, 6 Nov 2026)
- Powers: Commissioner of Police confers police powers on APOs, only while on duty, no investigation powers. [search summary only] https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL-Supp/S626-2004/Published/20060831?DocDate=20041011
- A ministerial speech says APOs have arrest powers and may bear firearms while on duty. [search summary only] https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/second-reading-of-the-police-force-amendment-bill-speech-by-mr-teo-chee-hean-deputy-prime-minister-coordinating-minister-for-national-security-and-minister-for-home-affairs/ (date and whether it took effect: not verified)

**Armoury in-charge** (APO unit). Appears in a 2023 case where a former APO in charge of an armoury was jailed for discharging a revolver. [search summary only] https://www.itiger.com/news/2549753724

**SAF range roles** (conducting officer, safety officer, ammunition in-charge): NOT found in any public MINDEF or SAF source. The search returned only US, Australian and Hong Kong range manuals. I will not present those as SAF doctrine. The team should verify the exact SAF titles with a DSO mentor or an NS-serving contact rather than quote them.

## The security process, step by step, with its legal basis

### A. Armed APOs (outline only; the detailed procedure is not public)

From the Minister's written reply of 10 May 2021 [fact] https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-the-issuing-of-firearms-to-police-officers-and-auxiliary-police-officers-by-mr-k-shanmugam-minister-for-home-affairs-and-minister-for-law/ :

1. Officers undergo security vetting before employment.
2. Firearms are kept in armouries.
3. At issue, systems verify the officer's identity and that he or she is authorised to carry firearms for that deployment.
4. After the deployment, systems track the timely return of the firearms.
5. These processes are regularly audited.
6. Officers can be barred from drawing firearms in some circumstances (for example under disciplinary investigation).
7. Whether geo-fencing or similar technology is used was not disclosed, "citing operational security" and saying such measures are not infallible.

From the 2024 police release [fact]: the APO's "service revolver, ammunition and baton" are drawn from and returned to the armoury; the employer (Certis) noticed the non-return and alerted police.

Annual certification before drawing firearms: seen in a search snippet only, and the MHA reply page did not mention it as a separate step. [search summary only] Treat as unverified.

Legal basis found:
- Police Force Act 2004, Part 9 and the Auxiliary Police Forces Regulations: each APO is supplied by the employer with batons, arms, ammunition and accoutrements as the Commissioner approves, to be used as the Standing Orders provide. [search summary only] https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/PFA2004-RG2?DocDate=20250731&ViewType=Pdf&_=20250731185021
- Arms and Explosives Act 1913: defines an "authorised person" as an auxiliary police force authorised by the Licensing Officer to accept deposits of guns, arms or explosives. [search summary only] https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/AEA1913
- The WTO record says the Arms and Explosives Act is to be replaced by the Guns, Explosives and Weapons Control Act 2021, and Guns Regulations dated 2025 appear on a mirror site. [search summary only] https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/arms-explosives-and-explosives-precursors. [inference] the new Act is probably now in force; verify before quoting any section number.
- Arms Offences Act 1973, s 3(2), unlawful carrying of a firearm: 5 to 14 years' imprisonment and at least six strokes. [fact] police release above.
- Whether a statute or the licence conditions require a logged draw and return: not found. The MHA reply describes the process as company and police systems, audited, not as a named legal provision.

Where APOs guard: Changi Airport is confirmed by the 2024 police release [fact]. Woodlands Checkpoint appears in a news headline about a man jailed for trying to touch an APO's gun. [search summary only, headline] https://thesun.my/malaysia-news/m-sian-working-in-sg-jailed-for-trying-to-touch-auxiliary-officer-s-gun-at-woodlands-checkpoint-no14293806/ . Jurong Island, protected places, and other posts: not found.

## The current solution and its gaps

What exists:
- Singapore APOs: armoury plus identity and authorisation check at issue, tracked return, regular audits. [fact] MHA written reply, 10 May 2021 (URL above). The 2024 case shows the return tracking worked as an alarm: the employer noticed the non-return and called police. [inference from the police release]
- Vendors (generic, none shown to be used in Singapore):
  - STid BE.WEAPON: UHF RFID and IoT armoury system; claims use in more than fifty police and military armouries and a 50% cut in check-in/check-out time. Vendor claim. [search summary only] https://www.stid.com/images/presse/Article-de-presse-EN/Dossier_Press_02_2021_ISJ_web_US.pdf (the fetch found no BE.WEAPON detail)
  - GOBO Systems RFID Weapon Tracking and Armory Management: central records, controlled check-in/out, chain-of-custody reports, RFID and barcode/QR. [search summary only] https://www.g2.com/sellers/gobo-systems
  - ARMS RFID, Cenango, Traceability Solutions: listed as armoury platforms on a directory. Marketing text, unverified. [search summary only] https://us.fitgap.com/search/armory-management-software/enterprise
  - A US State Department RFID pilot (2010) reported that tracking accuracy at police armouries could be as low as 50 percent. [search summary only] https://www.rfidjournal.com/?p=83595
- Armoury logbooks and ammunition accounting forms: no Singapore document found.

Gaps (all [inference], no source states them):
1. These systems are centred on the armoury door. Between issue and return, nobody records who physically holds the weapon if it is handed on.
2. A handover between two people away from the armoury has no signed, two-party record in anything I found.
3. Offline behaviour of the vendor systems: not found. The reference architecture mentions "application synchronization" for handhelds, but nothing on conflicts between two offline handhelds. [search summary only] https://www.southwestsolutions.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/rfid-armory-management.pdf
4. Detection is late: in 2024 the alarm came after the shift, and the item was out for the whole duty period.

## Where handovers happen, and why they're offline

Honest answer: not established.
- Singapore APO posts and SAF ranges: no source gives connectivity details. Not found.
- [inference] Ranges and training areas are the likeliest places for a handover away from a central register: ammunition goes from the depot to a range ammunition point, then to the firer, then spent cases and unfired rounds come back. This is a chain of several named people over one day, in forest or shielded sites, and sometimes overseas.
- [inference] APO sites (airport, checkpoints) have good coverage. Their handovers are weapon to armoury, not weapon to weapon, so there is little for the chain to add.
- The pitch should not claim "no signal at the airport". Use: "offline-first because the armoury server may be unreachable, range sites may have no coverage, and the record must still be signed at the moment of handover".

## How our concept applies

- Handover chain: armoury issues the revolver to Officer A (both sign). If A hands it to B at a post, both sign by QR and the server later rebuilds the chain. A double handover (same weapon to two people) is proven at sync.
- Items: serialised firearms (discrete), sealed ammunition boxes or pouches (tamper-evident seal), magazines. Each has a tag or seal ID to scan.
- Seals: for ammunition, a seal number on a sealed pack gives a clean "issued sealed, returned with seal intact or broken" record. For loose rounds and spent cases, a count is signed by both sides (the chain proves the count was agreed, not that the count is right).
- Range chain: depot in-charge to range ammunition in-charge to firer; at end, firer to in-charge with a signed count of rounds and cases returned.
- What it does not do: it does not stop theft or misuse, and it does not detect a non-return unless the server sees the shift end with no return handover.

## The defence or DSO link

- Ammunition and arms custody is core SAF business. MINDEF calls ammunition theft "very serious" and a Military Court of Appeal benchmark sentence exists. [fact] https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/23aug19_mq/
- DSO-specific link: not found. [inference] a DSO judge will see custody integrity (who held what, provable without a network) as a general defence problem, and may value the signed chain more as a pattern than as a firearms product.
- APOs sit under Home Affairs, not MINDEF. The defence link depends on choosing the SAF range scenario.

## One demo moment

A range at the end of the day, both phones in aeroplane mode. The range in-charge hands a sealed box of 100 rounds to a firer. Both sign by QR, and the firer scans the seal. At the end the firer returns the box with an agreed count and a signed note. The phones go back online and the server shows the full chain. Then show a second, conflicting handover of the same box to a third person, and the server names who made the double handover. Use dummy items and a fictional unit name.

## Weak spots and sensitivity

- Offline is the weakest link. Singapore is well covered, and the APO model has no person-to-person step. A judge may ask "why not just use the armoury system?"
- The 2024 and 2021 cases are not handover failures. Do not say the concept would have prevented them. At most: it would have shown who held the weapon at each moment.
- Weapons are sensitive. MHA itself declined to say what tracking it uses, for operational security. Do not imitate real SAF or APO procedures, forms or photos. Use dummy items and say clearly that this is not a real procedure.
- Do not name the officers in the court cases. The 2024 case has a mental-health and self-harm context in the reports; do not make it a punchline.
- Do not imply that Certis, AETOS or SAF failed. The sources say the opposite for 2024: Certis noticed and called police.
- Many facts here are [search summary only]. Verify the main ones before putting them on a slide.
